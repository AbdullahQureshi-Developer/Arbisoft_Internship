 Embeddings are a type of word representation that allows words with similar meanings to have a similar representation. They are a crucial part of many natural 
language processing (NLP) models, especially those based on deep learning.

In simple terms, an embedding is a numerical vector that represents each word in the vocabulary of the model. These vectors capture semantic properties of words, such 
as their meaning and context, allowing the model to understand the relationships between words.

Embeddings are learned during the training process of an NLP model. The goal is to minimize the distance between vectors representing words with similar meanings 
while maximizing the distance between vectors representing dissimilar words. This is often achieved using techniques like word2vec, GloVe, or FastText.

Now, let's talk about RAG (Recurrent Attention Gates). RAG is a mechanism used in some NLP models to improve their performance and flexibility. It combines the ideas 
of recurrence (the ability to maintain an internal state across time steps) and attention (the ability to focus on relevant parts of the input) to better capture the 
context of words in a sentence.

RAG is particularly useful in tasks like machine translation, where it can help the model to pay more attention to certain words or phrases that are important for 
understanding the meaning of the sentence. It does this by assigning different weights (or gates) to different parts of the input based on their relevance to the 
current state and task at hand.

In summary, embeddings are word representations used in NLP models to capture semantic properties, while RAG is a mechanism that combines recurrence and attention to 
improve model performance by focusing on relevant parts of the input.
